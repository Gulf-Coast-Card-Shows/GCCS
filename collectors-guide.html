The Collector's Guide

Quick-hit tips for your next show — what to pack, how to talk price, how to tell a slab from a fake, and the lingo so you're not standing at a table nodding along to words you don't know yet. No essays, just the good stuff.

Before you walk in

🎒 Pack Your Bag Right

  • Cash and your payment apps, both ready to go. Most tables take either these days — see the money section below for the details.
  • A couple of top-loaders and penny sleeves for anything you pull or buy raw on the spot.
  • Something rigid — a binder page, a card saver — if you're bringing your own stuff to sell or trade.
  • An actual want list, written down. "Looking for the alt-art from the last main set, will pay near TCGplayer low" gets you an answer. "Something from that one set" gets you nothing.

Money talks

💵 Talking Price Like You Mean It

  • Cash and digital both work at most tables now. CashApp and Venmo are everywhere, and a lot of vendors run Square for cards — don't assume it's a cash-only room.
  • Signal can still be genuinely bad at fairgrounds and expo halls, though. Keep some cash as backup in case an app won't load right when you need it.
  • Small bills help everyone, cash or not — a vendor digging for change on a $9 sale at 9am slows the whole line down.
  • Negotiating is normal, not rude. "What's your best on this?" or "can you do X if I take two?" always lands fine.
  • Don't insult the card to talk the price down — calling a clearly-NM card "basically damaged" doesn't work, vendors have heard it a thousand times, and it just burns goodwill.
  • Bundle instead of haggling one card at a time. A vendor would rather move three cards at a fair price than argue fifty cents on one.
  • Know your number before you open your mouth. Pull actual sold listings, not "buy it now" asking prices — those are often just somebody's hope.

Slabs, explained

🏅 Raw, Slabbed, and Everything Between

  • Raw = ungraded. Just the card, maybe sleeved.
  • Slabbed = sent to a grading company, authenticated, sealed in a hard plastic holder with a number on it.
  • PSA — the name everyone's heard of. Cost and turnaround depend entirely on the service tier you pay for.
  • BGS/Beckett — gives subgrades (centering, corners, edges, surface), so a 9 built from four 9.5s reads differently than a flat 9 elsewhere.
  • CGC — newer to cards specifically, often cheaper and faster, big with modern Pokémon.
  • SGC — shows up most on vintage sports.
  • None of these is "the best." Just different reputations by hobby and era — PSA still commands a premium on vintage/sports even against an identical-condition CGC card.
  • Thinking about submitting your own? Factor in the fee, the wait (weeks to months), and the real chance it comes back lower than you hoped. Most regulars only submit stuff they're already fairly sure is a 9 or better.

Protect your wallet

🔍 Don't Get Burned

Fakes and reprints exist, especially on anything hyped — vintage Charizard, big rookie patches, first-edition holos. Five-second checks that save you real money:

  • Weight and feel. Most fakes feel thin or too slick. Real cardstock has heft.
  • Back print quality. Blurry text or slightly-off color on the back is a tell.
  • The light test (Pokémon especially). Hold the card up to a light. Real ones have a black layer in the middle that blocks light. A lot of fakes you can see straight through.
  • Cert number lookup. For anything already graded, look the cert number up on the grading company's own site before you pay, not after.
Real talk: if a vendor gets weird about you checking a cert number or looking closely at a card, that's the red flag — not you being difficult.

Read the label

📋 Condition Cheat Sheet

Vendors throw around abbreviations assuming everyone already speaks it. Here's the quick key:

  • M / Gem Mint — flawless. Sharp corners, no print lines, basically fresh out of a pack.
  • NM (Near Mint) — looks perfect at a glance, maybe the faintest whisper of wear if you really hunt for it.
  • LP (Lightly Played) — visible but minor. Small edge wear, maybe a soft corner.
  • MP (Moderately Played) — noticeable wear, light scratches, some corner rounding. Still fully displayable.
  • HP (Heavily Played) — been through it. Creases, heavy whitening, still identifiable and often still cheap and usable.
  • Damaged — water damage, tears, writing, missing pieces.
  • Whitening — edges or corners worn down enough to show the white card stock underneath the colored border. The single biggest visual tell on darker-bordered cards.
  • If a vendor just says "played" with no other qualifier, ask which tier they mean — that word alone covers a lot of ground.

Don't Be That Guy (or Gal)

🤝 Table Manners

  • Ask before you touch. Vendors often have a system to their case, and random hands moving things around isn't it.
  • Digging through a dollar box? Put things back roughly where they were.
  • If you're not buying, don't camp a table for twenty minutes while other people are trying to get to the case.
  • A no on a trade is a no. It's their table, their call, no hard feelings needed.
  • If a kid's mid-excitement asking a vendor about a card, let the moment happen — even if it costs you thirty seconds of a negotiation. That's the whole point of these shows.
  • This community is smaller than it feels. Word gets around fast about who's good to deal with — and who isn't.

Know your game

🃏 Notes By Hobby

Every table on the floor speaks a slightly different dialect. A quick primer so you don't get lost:

Pokémon

Chase cards move fast — alt-arts and a fresh set's box-topper especially. "Hit" means something actually worth money, not every rare qualifies. Special-pattern reprints (master ball and similar) can spike overnight, so a binder specifically for those is common at bigger tables.

Sports Cards

RC = rookie card (a player's first licensed card). Auto = autographed. Patch/relic = a swatch of game-used material embedded in the card. Numbered (like /99) tells you the print run. A rookie patch auto (RPA) on a hot prospect can be the card of someone's whole day.

Magic: The Gathering

"Playset" = four copies, the max most formats allow in a deck. Foil carries a premium over non-foil of the same card. Commander/EDH staples hold value even on cards that look unassuming to anyone who doesn't play.

Yu-Gi-Oh, Lorcana & One Piece TCG

Newer to a lot of Gulf Coast tables but growing fast. Fewer vendors specialize, so pricing swings more table-to-table — five minutes of price research before you go is where this pays off most.

Comics & Toys

"Key issue" means a first appearance or otherwise story-important issue — that's what drives price, not just age. Raw comics use the same condition language as cards (NM, VF, FN), and CGC slabs comics the same way it slabs cards now.

Family day

👨‍👩‍👧 Bringing the Fam

  • Card shows are honestly one of the better family outings in the hobby — most organizers welcome kids.
  • Plenty of vendors keep a cheap bin specifically so a $5 bill turns into a real find.
  • Aisles get tight once the doors open — plan ahead if you're bringing a stroller.
  • Set the budget with your kid before you're standing at a table. Way less friction than negotiating it in the moment.
  • Let them do the talking if they want to. Vendors light up for a genuinely excited kid, and it's good practice for next time.

Keep your head

🎯 Set a Lid Before You Walk In

  • Decide your number before you're standing in front of a case with your heart rate up. The thrill of the hunt is exactly what blows budgets by the third lap around the room.
  • Track what you're spending as you go — easy to lose count once it's split between cash and a payment app or two.
  • Some regulars still go cash-only, no apps, for themselves specifically, because it forces a hard stop when the cash runs out. Worth trying once even if you normally pay digital.
  • Know what you actually walked away with versus what you told yourself you'd spend going in.

Talk the talk

📖 Lingo Cheat Sheet

Quick reference for the words you'll hear at every single table, all day.

PC
Personal collection — cards you're keeping, not selling.
Grail
The card you'd never actually sell, even for a dumb offer.
Hit
Something you pulled that's genuinely worth money — not just "a rare."
Whale
Someone spending big, often on singles or a box/case break.
Flip
Buying something specifically to resell for profit, usually fast.
Rip
Opening packs or boxes — "let's rip a box."
Box / Case Break
A group buy where a box or case is opened live and slots are split among buyers beforehand.
Slab
A graded card, sealed in its hard plastic holder.
Raw
Ungraded — the opposite of slabbed.
LGS
Local game/card shop.
Meta
Whatever's strong or popular in competitive play right now.
Bulk
Common, low-value cards typically sold by the pound or in big lots.
